When buying silver or gold bullion, authenticity is everything. Counterfeits can look convincing, but a careful buyer has several tools and methods to confirm a coin or bar is genuine. In this article, we’ll go through a combination of professional and simple at-home techniques—using Canadian Silver Maple Leafs as an example—so you can protect your investment.

### 1. Weight Test

**Example:** In the image above, a 1 oz Silver Maple Leaf is placed on a precision digital scale. The reading shows **31.102 g**, which matches the official specification for a 1 troy ounce coin.

* **How to do it:**

  * Use a digital scale with at least 0.01 g accuracy.
  * Compare the reading to the official mint specifications. Our Precious Metals Manager app also offers a catalog with the weight, but it's recommend to double check this measure with the official mint site.

* **Pros:**

  * Quick and inexpensive.
  * Easily rules out obvious fakes.

* **Cons:**

  * Some counterfeit coins use tungsten or other metals to match weight.
  * Must be paired with other tests for certainty.

---

### 2. Dimensions Check

**Example:** In the image above, the caliper shows the diameter of the coin as **38.0 mm**, matching the Royal Canadian Mint’s specifications.

* **How to do it:**

  * Use a digital caliper to measure diameter and thickness.
  * Compare with official mint data.

* **Pros:**

  * Detects coins that are overweight or underweight due to incorrect dimensions.
  * Low cost.

* **Cons:**

  * Skilled counterfeiters may match both weight and size.
  * Requires accurate measuring tools.

---

### 3. Precious Metal Verifier

**Example:** In the image above a [Sigma Metalytics](https://www.sigmametalytics.com/products/pmv-original) is used. It uses electrical resistivity to confirm the coin’s composition without damaging it. The readings fall within the bracket for **.9999 fine silver**, confirming authenticity.

* **Pros:**

  * Non-destructive, reliable, and fast.
  * Works through packaging, so you can test sealed bullion.

* **Cons:**

  * High initial cost.
  * Less effective on very small or oddly shaped pieces without wands.
  * Harder to carry around

---

### 4. Magnet Test

Silver and gold are non-magnetic, so a strong neodymium magnet can help detect counterfeits.

* **How to do it:** Tilt a coin and slide the magnet along its surface. Real silver will cause the magnet to slide slowly due to **eddy currents**, not stick or drop instantly.

* **Pros:**

  * Cheap and easy.
  * Detects many plated steel fakes instantly.

* **Cons:**

  * Won’t detect high-quality tungsten-based fakes.
  * Requires a strong magnet.

---

### 5. Ping (Sound) Test

When tapped, pure silver and gold coins produce a clear, ringing tone. Counterfeits often sound dull.

* **Pros:** Free, quick, and surprisingly effective.
* **Cons:** Requires a trained ear and practice.

---

### 6. Visual Inspection

Look for:

* Sharp, high-quality details in the strike.
* Correct font, alignment, and spacing.
* Surface finish consistent with mint-issued coins.

Using a loupe or microscope can reveal surface inconsistencies that betray a fake.

---

### 7. Advanced Methods (for high-value verification)

* **XRF Analyzer:** Identifies the exact metal composition. Very accurate but expensive.
* **Specific Gravity Test:** Compares weight in air and water to detect density mismatches.

---

### Recommended Approach

For maximum confidence, combine multiple methods:

1. **Weight + Dimensions** – catches most low-effort fakes.
2. **Magnet Test** – quickly eliminates plated steel fakes.
3. **Electronic Verifier** – confirms metal content.
4. **Visual Inspection** – final sanity check.

If you are buying high-value bullion, especially from an unfamiliar seller, consider having it professionally tested or only purchasing from reputable dealers.
